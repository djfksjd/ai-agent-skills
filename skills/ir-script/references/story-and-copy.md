# Story, copy and delivery playbook

These are proposed working rules synthesized for this skill. They are not empirical promises or mandatory universal IR rules. See `research.md` for inspected sources and their limits.

## Start with the decision, not the template

Ask: who needs to understand this, what can they already assume, and what should happen after the talk? A pilot invitation, a financing meeting and a public earnings update have different endings. A short live pitch is not a data room. A send-ahead deck needs enough explanatory context to survive without the speaker.

Draft a precise sentence: **buyer + job + product mechanism + resulting value**. Avoid opening with a generic industry forecast while the audience still does not know what the company does. A true founder origin can establish why this team cares; it is not customer validation by itself.

## Conditional narrative recipes

| Situation | Candidate order | Main evidence | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early product, limited traction | Product definition → origin and observed problem → alternative workflow → mechanism/demo → initial buyer/market → BM → evidence so far → acquisition/validation → team → ask | Original documents, interviews when actually conducted, tested prototype, explicit hypotheses | Fake traction; a long defensive status report |
| Strong operating traction | Product and strongest result → customer/use case → growth quality → acquisition/retention → market expansion → competition → economics → team/capital use → ask | Cohorts, paid conversion, customer concentration, unit costs | Vanity metrics pretending to be revenue |
| Deep technology/hardware | Customer bottleneck → measured problem → mechanism → test setup/results → manufacturability/cost → buyer/adoption → market → IP/competition → team/milestones → ask | Bench tests, units, boundary conditions, yield/BOM quotes | Decorative 3D, unlabeled simulation, invention of safety certainty |
| Competition or public program | Opening definition → problem → solution and feasibility → buyer/market → business model → execution/team → exact rubric coverage → closing | Event-specific rubric and requested scope | Assuming all Korean competitions use one order |
| Listed-company earnings | Reporting basis → highlights → business/segment drivers → financials/cash → guidance → Q&A | Reporting periods, definitions, reconciliations, actual/outlook distinctions | Importing this structure wholesale into a pre-revenue pitch |

Propose two reasonable orders, then choose one. Explicitly identify the slide that answers each audience question. Combine adjacent ideas when speaking time demands it. Do not turn every checklist item into a mandatory slide.

## Problem evidence ladder

1. Founder motivation: only what the founder actually stated.
2. Documented task: demonstrate the real workflow and source materials.
3. Reported pain: attributable interviews with participant scope and dates.
4. Measured cost: method, sample, baseline, distribution and limitations.
5. Verified improvement: same-task comparison including setup, learning and rework.

Do not jump levels. “The workflow needs three checks” does not mean “customers waste ten hours” or “competitors cannot handle it.” When evidence is missing, make the experimental question visible and keep the origin story intact.

## Competition and positioning

Compare direct competitors, substitute products, generic LLMs and manual work against the same customer job. Build a research matrix before designing the slide:

`provider / buyer / discovery method / recommendation evidence / preparation tasks / writing / output / revision+reuse / data path / price+billing period+VAT+seats / source date / tested vs advertised`

Only retain dimensions relevant to a purchase decision. Use actual descriptions, not self-awarded stars or an all-green row for your product. A feature not found in a reviewed page is unconfirmed, not absent. Verify exact package scope; annual effective monthly price is not a monthly cancel-anytime price. Label your product's planned functions consistently.

Form a testable position: **for this buyer, at this task, our mechanism may improve this metric**. Specify a comparison protocol rather than declare victory without testing.

## Market and BM discipline

Keep the initial paying buyer distinct from the user and beneficiary. Name the purchasing trigger, budget, alternative and paid period. Build market estimates from unique relevant buyers and a defensible price/usage assumption. Exclude non-paying populations and overlapping segments. If the denominator is unknown, show a scenario and the evidence needed to estimate it.

Map each tier to a buyer and paid job. Free can solve the entry task; paid must address an additional recurring job, usage or governance burden. Separate third-party subscription, your workflow fee, managed inference and institutional support. Do not force token/credit language onto a product whose business value is workflow automation.

Financial slide: include period and tax/currency basis. Distinguish customer targets from active customers, MRR from annualized MRR, annual prepayment from recognized revenue, and gross billings from contribution. Account for ramp, retention, actual inference costs, support, fees, payroll and acquisition or label exclusions. Sensitivity is usually more honest than a falsely exact break-even date.

## Korean copy pass

The objective is readable, precise writing, not proving whether a text was written by AI.

| Before | After | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 지원사업 준비 과정을 혁신합니다 | 공고 선택부터 제출본까지 | Replace abstract promise with the task |
| 고객이 비용을 절감할 수 있도록 합니다 | 재입력과 재검토가 생기는 구간 | Do not assert savings before measuring them |
| 가격과 비용을 구분했습니다 | 기능 구독료와 실행 비용 | Remove the editor's narration |
| 12개월 매출을 첫해 매출로 잡지 않습니다 | 첫해 누적 매출과 연 환산 매출 | Put the subject in the headline |
| AI가 아닌 진짜 파트너 | 담당자 검토가 필요한 항목 | Remove an empty contrast |

A concise statement can still be effective; do not blindly nominalize every headline. Keep a number only when it carries the page's point and its basis can be read. Avoid repeated '~합니다', unnecessary English labels, adjective stacks and ornate slogans. Preserve warnings that materially qualify a claim, while moving detailed methodology out of the main story.

Body copy should expose the process or evidence. A title is not the entire slide, and a script is not a transcript of the slide bullets. Do not compress the spoken script into broken noun fragments.

## Script composition

For each stable slide ID, write:
- What the audience should understand, in one sentence.
- Natural spoken explanation with the evidence level preserved.
- One specific visual cue and any pause/demo instruction, marked not for reading.
- A short transition that explains why the next slide follows.

Example, entirely hypothetical:

Slide title: **검토 의견에서 최종 문서까지**

Speech: “이 제품은 문서에 남은 의견과 반영 상태를 함께 보여주는 도구입니다. 담당자가 다음 작업을 확인하는 수고를 줄일 수 있는지, 같은 문서로 비교해 보려 합니다.”

Cue, not spoken: “의견 → 반영 상태 → 담당자 순으로 짚음. 화면이 설계 예시라면 실제 시연이라고 말하지 않음.”

Transition: “이 작업이 반복되는 고객부터 확인하겠습니다.”

Avoid repetitive '다음으로 설명드리겠습니다' on every slide. First-person experiences, test results and customer quotes must come from actual records.

## Timing and rehearsal

Treat timing rates as adjustable estimates. Korean speech may be estimated from characters and English from words, but both need real rehearsal. The compiler defaults to 5 non-space Korean characters/second or 130 English words/minute; these are editable planning assumptions, not universal rates. Count transitions and pauses. Exclude Q&A appendices from the main talk.

An optional eight-minute planning budget: definition/origin 45s, problem 45s, product/mechanism 90s, alternatives 45s, customer/market 45s, BM 45s, evidence 40s, acquisition/execution 45s, team/ask 40s. Total 480s. Reallocate to the actual argument; this is not an inferred order from a reference video.

During rehearsal record actual slide start/end times, long pauses, rushed passages and audience confusion. Trim low-value detail before speaking faster. Build a shorter cut by removing supporting examples, not changing facts or dropping essential qualifiers.

## Design pass

Follow user branding and template. Default to generous whitespace and a limited hierarchy. For live slides, start around 28–36pt titles and 18–24pt body text; adjust to room and density after rendering rather than treating these as standards. Use compact readable source notes, with full citations in notes/appendix.

Choose a visual appropriate to the question: customer workflow, product evidence, comparison table, bottom-up market tree, revenue bridge or milestone timeline. Avoid repeating the same cards on every page. Do not use a radar chart or subjective score to manufacture competitive superiority. Chart axes and units must be legible. Label real screenshots, mockups, simulations and measured results differently.

Use Three.js or another capable 3D renderer only for a physical mechanism that genuinely needs it. Render a clean image for the deck; do not assume interactive web elements will remain editable or work in PowerPoint. Logos/fonts/imagery require appropriate usage rights.

## Release checks

Content: no missing problem origin, buyer, alternative, market basis, business model, evidence or ask unless intentionally excluded with a reason. No invented customer stories or success claims.

Evidence: sources and dates next to important claims; numeric assumptions and financial arithmetic checked; competitor unknowns not marked absent; legal/security absolute promises removed unless substantiated.

Copy: every title checked; editorial comments separated from audience copy; speech sounds spoken; quotes accurate and brief.

Synchronization: actual counts and stable IDs across PPTX, PDF and script; appendix numbers match Q&A; selected input version named; hashes of outputs stored.

Visual: inspect every rendered slide and script page. Check overflow, missing Korean glyphs, unintended blank pages, labels, contrast and footnotes. Open in target PowerPoint/Keynote when available; otherwise state which renderer was tested.

Handoff: actual tests, untested platforms, unresolved facts, output links and a local state file. Publishing the skill does not mean a user's host has installed it.
